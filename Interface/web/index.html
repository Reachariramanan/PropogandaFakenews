      Case file

      How to read a case file

      Select a claim in the list to open its case file here. Use j and k or the arrow keys to move through the list and Enter to open. Each file has the same sections, in this order.

      1. Verdict and score. The fact-checker’s rating and the plausibility score (0 to 1) that the claim is fake or misleading, with how it was calculated.
      2. Why. Which other fact-checkers agree, and how many independent sources rated it.
      3. Evidence. The nearest earlier fact-checks, each with its similarity to this claim.
      4. Original text. An excerpt of the article or post that was checked.
      5. Signals. Wording cues such as capitals, exclamations and sensational terms. They describe style, not truth.
      6. Links. The original article or post.

      Scores are a triage aid. Always read the fact-checker’s own article before acting.

        Result

        No claim checked yet

        Paste a claim on the left, or pick an example, and choose Check claim. The result opens here in two steps.

        1. Step 1: Is it fake? Appears at once from the fact-check index, with a plausibility score and the nearest fact-checks. A verdict engine then adds its own opinion.
        2. Step 2: What kind of propaganda? Always shown, whether or not the claim looks fake: a probability bar for each of the 14 techniques, and the wording highlighted and labelled by technique.
        3. How we decided. Models, timings and the caveats, in a panel you can open.

        The first check of a new claim takes 20 to 35 seconds because the verdict engine reads it; repeats are instant. If nothing in the index is close, you get the engine's estimate, labelled as such.
